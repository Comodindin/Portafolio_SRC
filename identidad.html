Case study, 2D animated short

Problemas de Identidad

An animated short about trans youth in Mexico who live a double identity to protect themselves, and a call for the people around them to make room.

Team
Ángela Ximena Andrade Méndez, Saraí Rosas Chávez, Valeria Lorena Vargas Espinosa
Context
ASE I, Universidad Iberoamericana Puebla, 2022
For
Trans students aged 18 to 22
Built with
After Effects, Illustrator, Photoshop
Watch the short on YouTube ↗

The problem

In Mexico, many trans people face family and friends who don't accept their identity, even before any medical step. Stereotypes, prejudice and bullying at school push them to hide.

The result is a double identity: the one they discovered, and the one society expects, used as a defense against rejection and discrimination.

How can a short film build a community that gives emotional support to trans youth?

Research

Between August and September 2022 we interviewed three trans people and two specialists, an endocrinologist and a psychiatrist from a clinic for trans people in Mexico City, alongside secondary research on gender identity, hate crimes and how trans people readapt to society.

5 interviews: 3 trans users and 2 specialists

6 insight categories, merged into one

With users we used Creative Toolkit and Concept Mapping, so they could draw and map what they felt instead of only answering questions. Their drawings showed very similar feelings.

What we found

  • Hiding to survive Trans people often build a false identity to get by, especially at school, and it can take years to put a name to what they feel.
  • Family is the hardest part Across every interview, the family was the part of the situation that was hardest to face.
  • It starts early The specialists confirmed that being trans can be known from childhood, and that the false identity often starts then, out of fear of rejection.
  • Discomfort around 11 to 13 Discomfort with the body often begins in early adolescence, when sharing who you are would bring relief but fear of rejection wins.
Adapting shouldn't be one-sided: society also has to adapt to trans people, who have always existed.

Who it's for

Trans students aged 18 to 22 who are going through, or have gone through, a gender transition. Their main need is to be accepted and recognised, and they connect mostly through social media.

Insight: as teenagers, trans people want to feel comfortable in their bodies and show who they are, but fear of rejection and lack of support from their parents deepen the identity crisis.

  • Mildred, 22 Marketing student in Puebla, transitioning. Wants to use her field to raise awareness.
  • Camilia, 19 Trans woman and art student. Expresses herself through illustration and wants others not to hide.
  • Daniel, 18 From Puebla. Hides their identity from a family that dismissed it, and looks for community on TikTok and Instagram.

The personas are fictional, built from the interviews. Each one has a situation without the design and a scenario with it, for example finding the short through YouTube suggestions and joining a community around it.

The proposal

A 2D animated short that turns the semester's research into a call to the community, designed to live on a website with a forum and chat where people can share their experiences.

  • Legibility
  • Beauty
  • Visibility
  • Adaptability

Grounded in Henri Tajfel's Social Identity Theory and on gender dysphoria as described in the DSM-5.

Colour palette

Light pastel tones based on the trans flag. Blue scenes carry the protagonist's sadness and loneliness, pink brings sensitivity, yellow adds light, the psychologist's office is purple as a safe place, and the final room is blue and pink like the flag.

Production

  1. Script and storyboard

    The story follows a cycle we saw in most users, with an animatic as the visual guide.

  2. Characters and style frames

    Ian, their family and friends, designed to feel close. Style frames were revised several times to match the emotion of each scene.

  3. Animation

    Each of us animated scenes in After Effects, with mouth sprites for lip sync, and we solved puppet and frame problems together.

  4. Voices and sound

    Voice actors with dubbing training, recorded in the university's sound booths and mixed with the music.

  5. Feedback

    We showed the finished short to some of our users and gathered their comments.
